--- permalink: /research/ layout: page title: Research eyebrow: Research program lede: Mathematical neuroscience asking how structured perturbations reshape the behavior of nonlinear systems, and what moves a neural circuit from healthy to pathological activity. ---

The research draws on dynamical systems theory, chaos control, and data-driven modeling, working at scales from single neurons to large, heterogeneous circuits. Three connected directions share a common thread: models are built to match real data, analyzed with the tools of dynamical systems, and used to generate predictions that experiments can test. Specific projects below serve as examples of each direction; the underlying methods carry over to other circuits and to nonlinear systems beyond neuroscience.

Research with undergraduates

Every part of this program has room for students, with projects that start from where you are. With calculus and a bit of programming, you can simulate a model neuron, build a return map, or classify real spike trains within a semester, and then take on open questions through summer research or a senior thesis. Along the way you pick up numerical methods, dynamical systems, data analysis, and Python. Everything runs on a laptop, with no wet lab required. See current and past student projects →
